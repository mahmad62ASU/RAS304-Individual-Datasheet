Overview

The pitch-axis board controls the brushed DC motor for Team 103’s robotic arm. A PIC18F57Q43 Curiosity Nano receives commands from the team bus, reads motor current, and controls the DRV8871 H-bridge. The board can shut off the motor if it detects a fault.

Schematic Sections

SectionWhat it doesKey parts
Power and protectionThe 9–12 V input powers the motor driver. The regulator supplies 5 V to the control circuit.J1, F1, F2, D1, D2, U1 (LM7805T)
MicrocontrollerReads team commands and motor current, then sends speed and direction commands to the driver circuit.U2 (PIC18F57Q43 Curiosity Nano)
Motor driveThe H-bridge controls motor direction and speed. J6 connects the two motor wires.U3 (DRV8871), JP5, J6
Current measurementThe shunt measures driver return current. An amplifier and low-pass filter prepare the signal for the ADC.R3, U4A, R4–R6, C8
Fault shutdownA comparator detects overcurrent. The latch and logic gates block both motor inputs during a fault until the controller rearms the circuit.U5A–U8, Q1, JP4, JP6
Team connectionsJ3 carries team commands and the shared fault signal. J4 and J5 carry upstream receive and downstream transmit signals.J3–J5 (keyed 2×4 IDC)
Debug and expansionThe pushbutton sends a debug or arm request. The LEDs show power and status, and J2 exposes spare pins.SW1, D4–D7, J2

Design Notes

The current-sense amplifier has a gain of 21. R6 and C8 filter the signal at about 1.59 kHz before the ADC.

J4 and J5 use pin 1 for data and pin 8 for ground. Confirm the cable pinout with the team before hookup.
